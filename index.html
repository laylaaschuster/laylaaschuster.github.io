About

Background

I hold a PhD in Synthetic Biology from the Department of Microbiology and Cell Science, where my research focused on [PLACEHOLDER: brief description of your doctoral work — e.g., engineering gene circuits, characterizing metabolic pathways, etc.]. That foundation gave me a rigorous, mechanistic way of thinking about complex systems.

Over the past several years I have transitioned into computational biology, applying machine learning to biological data at a scale and resolution that wet-lab approaches alone cannot reach. My current focus is Explainable AI — developing methods that make model predictions interpretable in terms that are biologically meaningful, not just statistically convenient.

I believe that in biology, a model that cannot be interrogated is not yet useful science. My work sits at the boundary between building predictive systems and understanding what they have actually learned.

I am actively exploring research scientist and postdoctoral opportunities in both academia and industry, particularly at organizations where deep biological domain knowledge and computational rigor are both valued.
