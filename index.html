About

Background

I am a computational research scientist in the Department of Microbiology and Cell Science at the University of Florida. My PhD in Synthetic Biology focused on a problem central to bacterial engineering: how to reliably control gene expression across diverse species. I developed broad-host-range plasmid toolboxes for controlled gene expression, characterizing inducible and constitutive promoter systems across more than 15 species spanning three classes of Proteobacteria — generating one of the most comprehensive cross-species promoter sequence-activity datasets to date.

That experimental work surfaced a harder question: can we predict promoter activity from sequence, and does that prediction hold across phylogenetically distinct hosts? The complexity of promoter sequence space — and the nonlinear interactions among its elements — made clear that answering this would require machine and deep learning approaches that go well beyond traditional statistical modeling. Rather than hand that problem off, I deliberately retrained computationally to pursue it myself.

My current research applies machine learning and Explainable AI to understand the sequence determinants of promoter function across bacterial species. I am interested not just in building predictive models, but in understanding what they have learned. This drives my focus on XAI: developing interpretability methods that return biological insight, not just statistical attribution.

I am actively exploring research scientist opportunities in both academia and industry, particularly at organizations where rigorous biological domain knowledge and computational expertise are both valued.

Explainable AI Machine Learning Synthetic Biology XGBoost SHAP

Machine Learning-Powered Tools to Elucidate Species-Specific Promoter Design Rules Across the Proteobacteria

XGBoost classifiers trained on promoter sequence data across 15 Proteobacterial species achieved AUC >0.93, with SHAP analysis revealing that DNA physicochemical interdependencies are the primary species-specific determinants of promoter activity.

Predicting promoter activity from sequence remains unsolved, particularly across phylogenetically diverse bacterial hosts. We generated a comprehensive expression dataset by screening a constitutive promoter library across 15 species spanning Alpha-, Beta-, and Gammaproteobacteria, then encoded promoter sequences into biochemical, biophysical, and compositional feature vectors. XGBoost classifiers trained per taxonomic class using two-stage feature selection (mutual information + SHAP) achieved AUC >0.93 and F1 >0.82 across models. Explainability analysis identified autocorrelation of DNA roll and minor groove depth as top predictors — features encoding sequence interdependencies rather than individual positions — with distinct importance profiles across Proteobacterial classes.

Explainable AI Genomics XGBoost SHAP Plant Biology

Recombination and Chromatin Environment are Central Determinants of Subgenome Evolution in Polyploids

Across maize and Brassica rapa, SHAP interaction network analysis identified recombination rate as both the most influential and most interconnected predictor of subgenome dominance — accounting for up to 74% of total model SHAP values — despite showing no significant mean difference between subgenomes.

Polyploidy reshapes genomes through biased gene loss, but the drivers of differential subgenome fractionation remain poorly understood. We compiled 60 genomic and epigenomic features per gene in maize and 45 in B. rapa, then trained XGBoost binary classifiers to distinguish dominant from nondominant subgenome genes (AUC 0.64–0.92). Applying SHAP feature importance and interaction network analysis, we found that recombination acts as a meta-feature integrating chromatin accessibility, transposable element density, and active histone modifications — with gene chromosome location and histone marks H3K36me3 and H3K9ac consistently among the top contributors across both species.

Co-Lead Full Stack Development Database Architecture Entomology

Bark & Ambrosia Beetle Gallery

The world's largest curated database of bark and ambrosia beetle images and specimen data, aggregating contributions from the global entomology community into a single, searchable resource.

As co-lead full stack developer, I designed and built the frontend, backend, and database architecture for this community-driven resource maintained by the UF Forest Entomology Lab. The gallery consolidates images and specimen metadata contributed by entomologists and museum curators worldwide — sources that were previously scattered and inaccessible in one place — into an organized, searchable database with contributor attribution.

Other Explainable AI Contributions

Explainable AI Protein Structure Feature Engineering

InteracTor & SpheronizaTor — XAI Toolkits for Protein Structure Analysis

As a contributing author on both projects, I participated in developing two open-source XAI toolkits for profiling protein structure–interaction–function relationships. InteracTor applies feature engineering and SHAP-based explainability to protein interaction data (PLOS Computational Biology, 2025). SpheronizaTor introduces spherical voxelization for interpretable modeling of protein microenvironments (Computational and Structural Biotechnology Journal, 2026).
